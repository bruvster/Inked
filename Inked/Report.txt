INTRODUCTION
"Inked" is a simple database application program for a Tattoo Parlor Booking System that features an easy to use command line interface. Inked allows an employee to log in with their employee number on the command line, and the Inked experience from there will revolve around that employee. This booking system has been designed with the use case that the tattoo artists own the shop, and they do not have a receptionist. Because of this, they need an easy to use application that allows them to do most of the heavy lifting in their day to day operations. 
    
The shop has a consistent hourly rate of $150, and there is a 24 hour cancellation policy where a customer can cancel at any time, but within 24 hours of their appointment time they will have to pay a cancellation fee of $150 (1 hour of services). The shop is open every day from 9am to 5pm.

DATA DESCRIPTION
There are 4 classes/objects/relations in Inked: Customer, Artist, Tattoo, Appointment.
An Artist and a customer have a many-to-many relationship, which is consistent with tattoo shop business logic (a customer can be tattood by any artist, as many times as they want).
An appointment and a tattoo have a one-to-one relationship (however in the database they are seperate and logically connected by finding a date match between the two tables).
-->didnt want to have a foreign key here because the date VERY rarely might differ
A customer and a tattoo have a one-to-many relationship
An artist and a tattoo have a one-to-many relationship

APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS
The functionalities include:
-Keep a record of all customers of the shop, including ones who have cancelled appointments
-Book new appointments for customers, and keep track of their cancel date
-Add new customers, must be done before making a new appointment with a new customer
-Input tattoo info once completed, and generate a cost for the customer (lengthx$150)
-View all upcoming appointments (remember, centric to the logged in employee)
-View any potential touch up dates for tattoos (not appointments, seperate, free, informal)
-View number of tattoos a customer has had at the shop (in customer look up)
-View number of tattoos an employee has given (in employee + shop metrics)
 -View overall total revenue employee, and shop in metrics

That being said, I used my menu() function to ensure I developed in a modular fashion:
The menu() has switches for the user input that calls the different funcitons I implemented. I build the menu around the function calls as if they were working, and then I commented out the calls themselves. I then worked on implementing the functions one by one, uncommenting only that function call and doing my own exhaustive tests before moving on to the next function. This worked very well, and allowed me to compile very frequently throughout development. I never spent more than 3 mins at a time debugging due to this approach.
